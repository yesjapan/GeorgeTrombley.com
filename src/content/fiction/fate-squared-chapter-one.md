George was certain fate didn’t care about a person’s dreams, nor how hard they’d worked toward achieving them. And sometimes fate reminds you who’s in charge with a swift career-ending blow to your kneecap.

With a sigh, George clicked “Pay Now” and half of what he earned for the month transferred out of his bank account. After rent and utilities this would leave him with the bare minimum and a night out or two with Kim.

The loan management app, ironically called “Fate Management,” blared a trumpet sound as confetti showered the screen and a cheery voice exclaimed, “You’ve reached a milestone! It’s time to celebrate!”

George muttered under his breath, “Celebrate what? Being a slave to the bank?”

In a puff of smoke, a grim reaper appeared on the screen and spoke in an evil voice. “You did it. You killed your debt!”

A tombstone etched with the original amount of George’s debt poked up through the grass. The grim reaper raised its bony arms in the air, slashing a scythe diagonally through the tombstone. Blood filled the entire screen then rolled off with the new amount of $279,000, just under half of the original $562,000 debt George had been repaying years after his injury.

He drew in a deep breath and immediately exhaled, saying out loud to the empty room, “Another three years to go.”

Without looking or caring where it would land, he tossed his phone onto the battered leather sofa in his rundown one-bedroom apartment. Sunlight bouncing off the faded pink wall of the opposing building illuminated a single plant atop the shorter of two scuffed bookshelves filled with well-read books.

George didn’t mind having the worst view in the Flamingo Luxury apartment complex, since it meant cheaper rent. He guessed when the Flamingo was first built, “Luxury” was appropriate as part of the name. Young families and bachelors with dreams of ever-increasing property values rushed to buy units, but with the deterioration of the neighborhood and decades of poor management, calling the Flamingo apartments “Luxury” now felt like pure puffery. The Flamingo’s dream of a good life died long before George’s did.

George dumped the remainder of the organic soymilk Kim had brought over on their date night into the bowl of sweet-smelling, cheap, supermarket-brand cereal. Kim insisted he stop poisoning his body with hormone-laden cow milk. She was certain the government was spiking it with chemicals designed to keep the population meek—one of the many conspiracies she loved to talk about.

Reaching into the non-functioning dishwasher, which was still useful as a drying rack, he grabbed one of the three spoons he owned. Except for a single protein bar, this would be his only meal until after his final client’s training session ended at 7:00 p.m.

$600,000 in medical debt had kept George running on empty from the moment he was able to walk again. With his MMA career abruptly terminated, bankruptcy was his only option once his thoughts of suicide passed. But runaway capitalism had taken away the bankruptcy option with the enacting of the *Medical Responsibility Act*.

*Responsibility my ass. It shouldn’t cost over a half-a-million dollars to fix a busted knee over a single oversight.*

His insurance lapsed just three days prior to the law’s enactment. Who had time to open mail from insurance companies—even when marked as ‘urgent.’ They sat dumped in a box of mail he had planned to tackle after the fight.

George swished just enough water into the empty cereal bowl and spoon to clean them. Then, despite Kim’s insistence that he put things “where they belong,” he tossed them back into the dishwasher on the top rack as usual.

A quick glance at his refurbished vintage Apple Watch told him he still had an hour before his first of five booked training sessions. By stacking back-to-back clients every day of the week ending in “y,” he somehow managed to pay down half of the debt in just over two and a half years.

Once Kim made it clear she was sick of rushing to meet him in between sessions, for what she called “a standby date,” he began taking Thursdays off. He made up for the lost income by swapping volunteer work at the shelter with more paid client sessions. Until the debt was gone, it had him by the proverbial balls. With all that had derailed his life, paying down the debt was the only thing George felt he had control over.

The Thursdays set aside just for Kim ended up being therapeutic. George gained a brief respite from his overly packed schedule and the time with Kim gave him a mental place to forget the rich, entitled elites who insisted on a human trainer. They use these trainers as a status symbol despite the existence of capable, if not better than human, AI trainers.

Most Thursdays ended with George nestled next to Kim on his sofa watching true crime shows on a three-decade old Blu-ray player he purchased from a pawn shop. The player didn’t have a remote, but it would be a miracle to find one that did.

George opened a bottle of green tea from the fridge and flipped to the dog-eared page of his latest thrift shop find—the 1951 print edition of “Classic Crimes” written by William Roughead. Like all the books on his shelves, the book had seen better days. Most of the older books were available digitally, but digital books didn’t offer the same escape or privacy of the physical relics. The gritty feel of book pages sliding together upon turning was something George considered a mini nirvana moment.

A chime coming from the direction of the couch interrupted his reading. Swiping to the message, George sighed. *Not again. What’s wrong with this guy?*

George had already declined three previous training requests. From this guy’s over-the-top profile to his three-and-a-half-star rating, everything about him screamed, “pain in the ass.” And on top of that, the guy was insisting on a Thursday session, despite every Thursday on George’s schedule being marked as “unavailable.”

*I sure as hell don’t want to train on my Kim day. Not for another rich asshole.* George was ready to decline yet again, if not for the message attached to the request. The first sentence was in all caps.

READ BEFORE YOU DECLINE. I require a trainer with your attributes for a year-long unique project. I will pay three times your normal hourly rate for a guaranteed minimum of three hours a week. Cash will be paid discreetly at the end of every session.

*Three times my rate! Why?*

It seemed too good to be true. He reread the message to make sure he hadn’t misread. He could hear Kim’s conspiratorial voice in his head saying, “There must be a catch.”

He had questions.

*Who is this guy? Why spend so much? What’s so special about me? And why pay discreetly?*

George calculated that by adding this job to his schedule, he could shave half of the remaining time off of his loan repayment and rid his life of the constant reminder of his failed MMA career. He would finally feel comfortable proposing to Kim. Although she wouldn’t be happy about going back to “standby” dates, he hoped she would understand.

*I guess the fourth time’s the charm.* He clicked the “Accept Client” button, and the training session was added to his calendar. Now for the hard part. He tapped Kim’s name in the messaging app and began typing.

```chat me=George
George: Hey, I won’t be able to make our Thursday lunch this week.
Kim: Oh?
Kim: Anything I should know about?
George: Nothing serious, I accepted a new client who insists on Thursdays.
Kim: We discussed this George…
Kim: Thursdays are for us. 😢
George: They are, but this guy was persistent, and he offered triple my normal rate.
Kim: Triple??
Kim: As in $600 an hour???
```

George didn’t talk much about personal issues with Kim, especially his financial difficulties, so she wasn’t aware of his large medical debt. However, at one time she had been a paying client, so she was aware of his previous rate of $200 per hour.

```chat me=George
George: Actually, I raised my rate to $250 last month.
George: So, $750.
Kim: WOW! Who is this guy? And does he need a young, beautiful, go-getter PR agent?
George: I’m not sure who he is besides his name.
George: I’m meeting him on the 200th floor of that LETR building, so I assume he’s some high-up executive there.
Kim: Are you kidding me? LETR? I strongly recommend you __DO NOT__ take this job.
George: Why?
George: Are you saying $750 an hour isn’t enough?
Kim: No, not that. Give me a second to look for something.
```

The only thing George cared to read were his true crime books and training requests. Consequently, news of the world came to him primarily via conversations with Kim, whose work required her to be hyperaware. George knew LETR had built a skyscraper in the center of the city but didn’t know much beyond that.

Shin Hye Kim, a second-generation Korean, went by her last name “Kim” primarily because non-Koreans always messed up her first name, Shin Hye. Her work as a public relations manager meant when she wasn’t creating stunts to get publicity, she was scouring social media, news articles, and watching exposé videos. After a few moments, Kim sent two links.

```chat me=George
Kim: Check these out. There’s more, but this should give you an idea of why LETR is so dangerous.
```

The first link was to an article from the *Texas Daily Telegram* titled “LETR Under Fire for Suspicious Deaths at Dallas Facility.”

An anonymous whistleblower working for LETR, at their now shuttered research facility in Dallas, claimed upwards of a hundred chimpanzees and bonobos had died under mysterious circumstances, and that LETR had covered up their deaths using an onsite incinerator to destroy the evidence. LETR of course denied the claims, and a subsequent investigation by the Dallas Special Investigations Bureau ended with no charges due to a lack of evidence.

Kim loved this type of large cover-up story the same way George got pleasure out of decades-old true crime stories. In regard to conspiracy, George tended to err on the side of doubt. Most conspiracies could be attributed to ineptitude and not malice. Still, he couldn’t help but enjoy how animated Kim got when she waxed on about her latest conspiracy discovery. His doubting nature made him the perfect devil’s advocate and led to stimulating debate between them.

The article about LETR wasn’t convincing.

To George, it seemed more plausible that a disgruntled employee leaked the fantastic story for some sort of revenge. Or maybe it was part of a scheme to blackmail LETR. Although, it’s curious as to why a tech company would need an onsite incinerator.

The second link was to a video titled “The Many Evils of the LETR Corporation.”

It was a well-produced video released a few days ago on the popular *The Truth Wants to Be Free* channel, known for its viral videos exposing the supposed wrongdoings of government agencies and large companies. It claims to be the largest private investigative organization in the world, with thousands of anonymous investigators. Despite their grandiose claim, George had never heard of the group.

The video opened with dramatic music and fancy motion graphics. A map popped up showing hundreds of factories, research facilities, and office buildings owned by LETR throughout the world. George raised his eyebrows at the sheer number of locations. It seemed LETR was everywhere.

*Is LETR really this big?*

The narrator spoke. “This behemoth of a company, so intertwined in our daily lives, has no accountability to the billions of people interacting with its products every day. Its repeated scandals are left unanswered and unpunished.”

Headlines of various scandals involving LETR flashed across the screen with the sound of thunder for each one.

The narrator continued, “LETRs response to these major scandals is to have yet another puppet CEO step down while paying them millions of dollars in undeserved golden parachute compensation. Then soon after, another figurehead CEO takes over making hollow promises of change to assuage the public outcry.”

George tried to think of any LETR products he owned, or had seen for that matter, but couldn’t immediately come up with any.

Most of the media he consumed were movies or documentaries on discs from thrift shops. The rare exception was a single broadcast TV show. He vaguely remembered the show running commercials bragging about LETR changing the world—possibly with medical scanners and maybe automation robotics.

Did billions of people interact with LETR every day? He wasn’t sure.

The narrator’s voice deepened slightly. “Now let’s examine three actions by LETR that are cause for concern.”

The next segment brought into doubt the circumstances surrounding LETR’s acquisition of a highly valued block of land in downtown San Diego. It was here they erected their flagship research facility, an impossibly tall skyscraper in the heart of downtown San Diego, named simply, The LETR Building.

It seems the owners of a long-held theater on the land where the building now stands, had refused to sell for years. The video cut to a series of news clips of the theater owner vowing to never sell.

“It’ll be a cold day in hell before I sell!”

In the final clip he shouted, “San Diego is better without LETR!”

Less than six months after screaming desperately into a news drone, “LETR will never get an inch of my theatre or land!” the building began construction. The narrator posed the question, “What tactics did LETR use to oust this owner from his property? And more importantly, why is the once vocal theater owner now silent?”

The next segment rehashed the suspicious deaths of the chimpanzees. Apparently, LETR had illegally imported the primates along with many other species using their extensive and, according to the booming voice on the video, “unchecked” trade routes.

But now instead of one hundred, the video claimed over one hundred and fifty chimpanzees and bonobos were *murdered* needlessly by LETR during unspecified research. The word “murdered” was sharply emphasized by the narrator.

This was the only point that felt substantial to George. Leaked by a disgruntled employee or not, if true, LETR should have been penalized, but the video offered no proof. LETR eventually admitted to a portion of the claim stating that while unfortunately a few chimpanzees had *expired* in their custody, their deaths were from natural causes.

The final segment felt forced to George. The narrator lamented over the fact that LETR, one of the largest companies in the world, had no legal obligation to be transparent with its financials since it was privately held.

The narrator asked, “If LETR has nothing to hide, then why not reveal their client list, how they are funded, and disclose who is actually in control? A company this size shouldn’t be allowed hidden agendas.”

George watched the rest of the video but was left unconvinced LETR was as evil as the narrator claimed.

On the surface, these questions, along with the ominous music, made it seem LETR was up to something nefarious. However, George reasoned it was in LETR’s best interest to keep their funding and client list private. This information would only serve their competitors a road map to erode LETR’s market position in whatever business they were doing, not that George knew what they actually did since the video didn’t say.

George thought, *not everything’s a conspiracy, sometimes it’s just business*.

He certainly wasn’t going to say no to their incredible offer based on so-called “evidence” that seemed more like opinion than fact. He would have to let Kim down softly. He messaged her.

```chat me=George
Kim: Right!? LETR is definitely up to no good! So, if we’re still on for Thursday, how about we grab a pizza and watch that justice show you like?
```

George initially typed “Sounds nice, but now I’m more curious about LETR than a crime show” but decided he needed a reason beyond mere curiosity or Kim might keep sending him links. He decided to tell her the truth instead.

```chat me=George
George: The money’s too good to refuse or else I would certainly cancel.
Kim: Don’t let the money blind you, George. LETR puts on a good face, but they’re up to no good. I’m sure of it.
```

Even if LETR was up to no good as Kim thought, George knew once he had paid off his debt and was kneeling in front of her proposing, it would all be worth it.

```chat me=George
George: I’m not blinded. But that money is pretty shiny. 🙂 Also, I’m not sure refusing the job would change much.
Kim: Capitalism at its finest. 😝
Kim: Just be careful, all right?
```

What he needed to be careful about eluded him. He wasn’t a chimpanzee or bonobo after all. But he knew Kim needed to be assured.

```chat me=George
George: Yeah, I’ll be careful. How about we meet in the evening for drinks? And of course, an episode or two of Citizens’ Justice would be perfect.
```

Although George didn’t drink much, he knew Kim loved a good drink to wind down after her typical busy day.

```chat me=George
Kim: I’m 100% up for drinks and socializing! And I’ve been waiting to take you to The Whiskey Den, so this is perfect.
```

George always let her pick the place.

```chat me=George
George: I’ve never been but, yeah, Whiskey Den sounds good.
George: I’ll text you when the session is done.
```

Kim sent a jumping rabbit animation showing her excitement.

```chat me=George
Kim: ❤ ❤ ❤ I know you’ve never been.
Kim: It’s so perfect for you. But hey, promise me you’ll be on guard at LETR.
George: Yeah, I promise. You know me, I’m always on guard and inherently trust no one.
```

Even George knew that wasn’t exactly true. While it’s true he distrusted new people, once you gained his trust, he often dropped his guard. Kim loved to tell him he needs to fear people the way deer fear hunters in the forest. It was the same warning she gave her clients under constant scrutiny from paparazzi and the unforgiving public gaze.

The rest of George’s day was uneventful except for Kim sending more links about LETR. This was typical for her. Once she got something in her head, she would relentlessly pursue it.

On any normal day, George wouldn’t click a news link even from Kim. He would rather read a jilted lover’s crime of passion than gossip masquerading as a news article, its sole purpose to invoke emotion in the reader. But Kim insisted George quote, “Know what you’re getting yourself into.” So in between training sessions, George sat in the back of transports catching up on Kim’s newest batch of blog posts from ex or anonymous employees, and exposés from journalists.

By the end of the day, he still didn’t know what to believe. The only thing clear to George was LETR was indeed affecting billions of people’s lives.

The final transport dropped George off in front of the Flamingo sign. The “o” in Flamingo and the entire word “Luxury” had been burned out for years, leaving perhaps the more appropriate name “Flaming Apartments.”

Back in the apartment, George dropped his equipment in its normal spot near the door. He was too mentally and physically tired to wipe it down like usual. It would have to wait until tomorrow morning.

After brushing his teeth in the kitchen sink which, unlike the bathroom one, didn’t occasionally spit out brown water, he took a cold shower, crawled in his bumpy bed and began reading the second to the last chapter of the novel *Classic Crimes*. It was difficult to read as the author was Scottish and had a tendency to make up words. In an empty room George said “perfidy” aloud, wondering if it was a real word. He’d look it up tomorrow if he remembered.

A ping from his phone interrupted his reading, notifying him of a new message. Pulling the phone off the charger he immediately saw it was Kim sending him yet another conspiracy link about LETR. He tapped the sleep-mode icon and the phone’s screen turned dark*.*

*No more LETR links today.*

He told himself it’s just a job and Kim was overreacting. But she had sent so many links that he was beginning to overthink the entire offer. Nothing about the job made sense.

*Why hire me? I’m not so special.*

George was one of over a hundred trainers on the SD Trainer’s App. If he were the top trainer, it might make sense that the man would be so aggressive in hiring him, but he wasn’t even in the top twenty for earnings or overall trainer rankings. He didn’t want to look this gift horse in the mouth. He just wanted to do the job, earn the money, pay off his debt, and propose to Kim—in that order.

*Damn it, Kim.*

He tossed the *Classic Crimes* on the nightstand and pulled the string on the lamp twice. The room was mostly dark except for the pale pink light of the Flamingo apartment’s neon bouncing off the neighboring building’s wall into his windows. He had one final thought before entering dreamland…

*Why is he offering to pay me three times my rate?*
